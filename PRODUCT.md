# Product

## Register
brand

## Product
Don Quijote de las Finanzas (QuijoteFinanzas)

## Market
Spain. Spanish-language personal finance, investing and economic education.

## Users
People who want to understand financial decisions before acting: savers comparing bank accounts and deposits, retail investors studying funds and portfolio construction, employees or households using financial calculators, and readers who want practical financial education without sales pressure.

Users may arrive from X, Instagram, Google, the newsletter or a shared calculator/comparison page. Many are not finance professionals. They need clarity, current data and explicit conditions more than financial jargon.

## Product Purpose
QuijoteFinanzas turns financial questions into practical tools, comparisons and explanations. The website is not a brochure for the social accounts; it is an owned product intended to provide recurring utility, organic discovery, newsletter acquisition and trustworthy reference material.

The product should help a user do at least one of these things quickly:
- compare financial products with conditions and limits visible;
- calculate a financial scenario and understand its assumptions;
- learn a concept in plain Spanish;
- inspect a real portfolio and the reasoning behind it;
- subscribe for useful project updates.

## Core Product Areas
- **QuijoCuentas:** current comparisons of accounts, deposits and promotions. Rates, dates, limits, requirements and deposit protection matter as much as the headline yield.
- **Calculators:** practical financial calculators with assumptions and limitations visible.
- **QuijoLista:** curated investment products/funds with identifiers, costs and sources where relevant.
- **QuijoCartera:** the creator's real portfolio, used as an educational case study rather than a model portfolio.
- **QuijoGlosario / resources:** educational reference content.
- **Newsletter:** owned distribution for meaningful new tools, comparisons and resources.

## Brand Personality
Clear, rigorous, useful, independent, calm and editorial.

Three-word personality: **rigorous, practical, human**.

The site should feel like a carefully edited financial publication that also happens to contain excellent tools. It should not feel like a bank, broker, affiliate funnel, generic SaaS landing page or AI-generated dashboard.

## Editorial Principles
1. **Accuracy before speed.** Current figures that can change must be verified before publication.
2. **Conditions before headlines.** A rate or return is incomplete without duration, limits, eligibility and relevant risks.
3. **Facts, opinion and assumptions are distinct.** Do not blur them.
4. **No universal recommendations.** Explain context and trade-offs.
5. **Utility before volume.** Do not add content or components because a landing page is expected to have them.
6. **Plain language without infantilising.** Reduce jargon but preserve necessary precision.
7. **No clickbait or artificial urgency.** Trust is more valuable than conversion tricks.

## QuijoCartera Rules
QuijoCartera is a real portfolio and must never be presented as a model to copy.

Public website rules:
- never expose absolute euro values of the portfolio or its positions;
- public composition is expressed in percentages;
- do not invent or display a risk-profile label such as "moderado" unless the product explicitly introduces one in the future;
- homepage/marketing screenshots should show composition, names/logos and weights only; do not fabricate returns, portfolio values or other metrics;
- use the latest internally verified portfolio snapshot and never mix dates.

## Monetisation and Trust
Referral/affiliate relationships must be identified. They must not determine the order of QuijoCuentas or the editorial selection of QuijoLista.

The site should make independence visible through methodology and disclosure, not through exaggerated claims such as "100% unbiased".

## Conversion Priorities
Primary conversions, in order:
1. useful interaction with a tool/comparison;
2. newsletter subscription;
3. return visit / exploration of another useful section;
4. contact or collaboration when relevant.

Do not sacrifice product clarity to maximise newsletter or affiliate clicks.

## Anti-references
Avoid:
- generic SaaS hero layouts with decorative badges, excessive pills and identical feature cards;
- purple/blue AI gradients, glassmorphism, neon glows and decorative blobs;
- emoji as the main visual language for navigation or feature headings;
- fabricated product screenshots or invented financial figures;
- testimonial cards with vague first names and no verifiable context;
- placeholder links, "coming soon" cards in prominent areas, and unfinished-template cues;
- repeated aphoristic copy such as "Sin X. Sin Y." when a concrete sentence is clearer;
- oversized conversion popups or artificial scarcity;
- dense dashboards on the homepage before users understand what the site offers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG 2.1 AA for core pages and interactions.

Commitments:
- semantic HTML first;
- keyboard access and visible focus states for all interactive elements;
- sufficient contrast in light and dark themes;
- respect `prefers-reduced-motion`;
- touch targets should remain usable on mobile;
- financial meaning must never rely on colour alone;
- inputs require clear labels/accessible names and errors must be understandable.

## Data Freshness
Any figure that can change (TAE/TIN, promotional deadlines, fees, tax rules, market data, fund costs, portfolio composition) must be dated and/or sourced appropriately. Old project documents are context, not current evidence.

## Success Criteria
A successful page is one where a user can answer:
- What is this?
- What can I do here?
- What are the important conditions or assumptions?
- How current is the information?
- What should I explore next?

without having to decode the interface or trust a promotional claim.
